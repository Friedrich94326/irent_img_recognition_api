Damage size, measured against a tyre

Damage model weights\car_damage.pt · tyre model weights\tyre_plate.pt · fee model weights\repair_fee_xgb.json (XGBoost, trained on simulated history) · generated 2026-09-26 01:26

A photo has no scale on its own, so a scratch box of 200 pixels could be a scuff or half a door. Every rental car photo also shows a tyre of a roughly known size, so we use the tyre as a ruler. Each detected damage is reported as a share of the tyre's area and as an approximate area in cm², and its severity is moved up or down by that size.

  1. Detect damageYOLO damage model: class, confidence and box
  2. Detect the tyresTyre + plate model; each damage uses the nearest wheel
  3. Tyre = 63 cm rulerLonger ellipse axis ≈ true diameter, even at an angle
  4. Size the damageBox area ÷ tyre area, and cm² from the pixels-per-cm scale
  5. Adjust severity< 5 % of tyre: one level down · > 25 %: one level up
  6. Merge & check for impactOverlaps counted once; a collision pattern makes the photo severe
  7. Estimate fee (XGBoost)Damage mix, sizes and impact → NT$; trained on simulated repair history for now

The cm² figure assumes the damage is about as far from the camera as the tyre, and it uses the box rather than the exact damage outline, so read it as an estimate.

How the fee model works

Past repair cases teach an XGBoost regression model what each kind, size and combination of damage costs; the model then prices any new photo from the same features. It is trained on simulated history until real repair invoices are exported.
